## Roll for Perception Check

Most of the systems in the deck were built for gameplay—grouping, ordering, matching, traversal, spatial interaction. The words and icons carry some meaning, but they were chosen to support those systems first.This was never the primary goal, but I always wanted some kind of casual inspirational use. Just need something to fiddle with and clear my mind? Flip a few cards and see what they say.

## A Multi-Axis Narrative Engine

One useful way to think about the deck is as a set of independent axes, with a simple way to map them when drawing cards.

Each drawn card answers one question:

- **Actor / Subject — What is happening**? → word
- **Domain — Where**? → suit
- **Behavior / Lens — How does it operate**? → sphere
- **Motivation — Why**? → mode
- **Scale — How far along / how big**? → rank (number)
- **Stage / Nature — What kind of moment is this**? → rank theme (second card)
- **Tone / Condition — Under what conditions**? → zodiac / planet

You don’t have to use all of these. The deck works best when you pick a subset and stay consistent.

Rank shows up twice in practice. One card gives you scale (early vs late), and another can give you the *type* of moment—signal, change, structure, etc.

---

### Example 1 — 3-card draw (fantasy)

**Question**: What is the central tension?

- **Actor**: 5 of Crescents — *Tether*
- **Lens**: 7 of Hearts — Air / Motion
- **Motivation**: 2 of Diamonds — *Confusion*

**Abstract read**:Connection or attachment, applied through thought or direction, driven by confusion.

This could be:

- being bound to an unclear path
- following guidance that contradicts itself
- holding onto something that no longer makes sense

**Concrete**:A mage is bound to a path of magic he no longer understands, following signs that keep changing as he reads them.

### Example 2 — 4-card draw (sci-fi)

**Question**: What is happening?

- **Actor**: 11 of Stars — *Omen*
- **Lens**: 5 of Leaves — Fire / Force
- **Motivation**: 14 of Diamonds — *Confusion*
- **Domain**: 4 of Spades — Spade suit

**Abstract read**:A signal or warning, acting with force, misunderstood, within an abstract or symbolic system.

This could be:

- a misinterpreted alert
- a system reacting incorrectly to input
- a warning escalating instead of resolving

**Concrete**:A warning signal pulses through an AI network, but the system misinterprets it and escalates the crisis it was meant to prevent.

### Example 3 — 5-card draw (historical)

**Question**: What is unfolding?

- **Actor**: 3 of Leaves — *Grain*
- **Lens**: 14 of Hearts — Chaos / Mystery
- **Motivation**: 7 of Leaves — *Transformation*
- **Scale**: 11 of Hearts — mid-high rank
- **Stage**: 12 of Flames — Craft

**Abstract read**:Something cultivated, disrupted by unknown forces, driven to change, already in progress, entering a stage of deliberate shaping.

This could be:

- a system breaking down and being rebuilt
- a natural process forced into transition
- a resource being transformed under pressure

**Concrete**:A harvest fails under strange conditions, forcing a village to rebuild how it survives before the next season arrives.

### Example 4 — 6-card draw (modern drama)

**Question**: What is the tone of this situation?

- **Actor**: 14 of Diamonds — *Moon*
- **Lens**: 11 of Stars — Chaos / Mystery
- **Motivation**: 2 of Hearts — *Instability*
- **Domain**: 14 of Diamonds — Diamond suit
- **Scale**: 14 — high rank
- **Tone**: 14 of Diamonds — Moon (planet)

**Abstract read**:Something symbolic or reflective, operating under uncertainty, driven by instability, in a personal or material domain, at a late stage, shaped by cycles or obscurity.

This could be:

- emotions surfacing unpredictably
- hidden information becoming relevant
- a situation that feels significant but unclear

**Concrete**:A late-night conversation spirals as hidden emotions surface, revealing truths that were always there but never clearly seen.

**Question**: Where and why?

- **Actor**: 4 of Spades — *Guardian*

- **Lens**: 1 of Spades — Earth / Form
- **Motivation**: 13 of Peaks — *Wonder*
- **Scale**: 6 — mid rank

### Example 6 — 4-card draw (adventure)

**Question**: What drives the journey?

- **Actor**: 7 of Hearts — *Arrow*
- **Lens**: 7 of Hearts — Air / Motion
- **Motivation**: 13 of Peaks — *Wonder*
- **Scale**: 7 — mid rank

**Abstract read**:Direction or intent, guided through movement or thought, driven by curiosity, at an active stage.

This could be:

**Concrete**:An explorer follows a direction that makes no practical sense, driven not by necessity, but by the need to see what lies beyond.

---

None of these are “correct.”

They’re just reasonable interpretations that fall out of the structure.

The important part is consistency: each role pulls from a real card, and each card contributes a single signal. You can extend the draw to add specificity, or reduce it to keep things open, without changing the method.

## Storyboarding (Icons Alone)

There’s another way this works that doesn’t rely on the axis system at all.

The icons all exist in the same visual world, and many of them relate to each other. Tools, structures, landscapes, processes—these show up repeatedly.

If you lay out cards and look only at the images, they often start to form sequences.

Sometimes this happens by chance in a random draw. Other times, you can construct it intentionally—picking cards that share visual elements or implied actions.

That makes the deck usable in a different way:

- as a sequence of events
- as a visual timeline
- as a storyboard

In this mode, the words and systems are still there if you want them, but they’re not required. The images carry enough continuity on their own to suggest progression.

---

A simple rule of thumb:

- **one card → idea**
- **several cards → system**

Because the axes are independent, meaning comes from interaction rather than prescription.
